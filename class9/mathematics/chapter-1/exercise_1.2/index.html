This exercise covers the fundamental rules for working with radicals and exponents. **How do we convert between radical and exponential forms?** You will learn the **conversion between radical expressions and exponential forms**: - Radical form: $$\\sqrt[n]{a^m}$$ (nth root of a raised to power m) - Exponential form: $$a^{m/n}$$ - Example: $$\\sqrt[3]{216^2} = (216)^{2/3}$$ **How do we apply product and quotient rules for radicals?** You will learn to apply the **Product and Quotient rules for radicals**: - **Product Rule**: $$\\sqrt[n]{a} \\times \\sqrt[n]{b} = \\sqrt[n]{ab}$$ (only when radicals have the same index) - Example: $$\\sqrt{4} \\times \\sqrt{8} = \\sqrt{32}$$ - **Quotient Rule**: $$\\frac{\\sqrt[n]{a}}{\\sqrt[n]{b}} = \\sqrt[n]{\\frac{a}{b}}$$ (only when radicals have the same index) - **Important Note**: Radicals can only be merged if they have the **same index**. Unlike exponents, the product rule for radicals requires identical indices. **What are the laws of exponents?** A significant portion of the exercise is dedicated to using **Laws of Exponents**: - **Product Rule**: $$a^m \\times a^n = a^{m+n}$$ (only applies to the same base) - **Quotient Rule**: $$a^m \\div a^n = a^{m-n}$$ - **Power Rule**: $$(a^m)^n = a^{mn}$$ - **Zero Exponent Rule**: $$a^0 = 1$$ (for $$a \\neq 0$$) - **Negative Exponent Rule**: $$a^{-n} = \\frac{1}{a^n}$$ (results in positive exponents in final answer) - **Fractional Exponent Rule**: $$a^{m/n} = \\sqrt[n]{a^m}$$ **How do we simplify exponential expressions?** You will learn to simplify complex numerical and algebraic expressions by: - Reducing bases to their **prime factors**: - $$216 = 6^3 = (2 \\times 3)^3 = 2^3 \\times 3^3$$ - $$32 = 2^5$$ - $$81 = 3^4$$ - $$125 = 5^3$$ - Ensuring all final results feature **positive exponents** (no negative exponents in answer) - Combining like terms and grouping similar bases **How do we solve algebraic proofs involving exponents?** The exercise includes advanced **algebraic proofs** where these laws are used to show the equivalence of multi-variable expressions: - Step-by-step demonstration using exponent rules - Grouping similar terms (same base) - Careful handling of negative signs outside radicals: Example: $$-(\\sqrt{6})^6 = -36$$ **Important Distinction**: The Product Rule for Exponents ($$a^m \\times a^n = a^{m+n}$$) only applies to the **same base**, whereas radicals can only be merged if they have the **same index**.

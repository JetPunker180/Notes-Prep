What is scientific notation? Exercise 2.1, "Scientific Notation," introduces the standard method for expressing very large or very small numbers in the form $a \times 10^n$, where the coefficient $a$ is between 1 and 10 ($1 \leq a < 10$).

How do we convert numbers to scientific notation? This exercise provides step-by-step solutions for converting standard decimals and large integers into scientific notation:

  • Large integer example: $53,400,000 = 5.34 \times 10^7$ (move decimal 7 places left)
  • Small decimal example: $0.00053407 = 5.3407 \times 10^{-4}$ (move decimal 4 places right)

How do we convert back to standard notation? You will also learn the reverse process of converting scientific notation back into ordinary/standard notation.

How do we simplify expressions using scientific notation? A significant portion of the exercise focuses on simplifying complex expressions involving products and quotients by converting into scientific notation and applying laws of exponents.

How do we solve real-world word problems? The exercise applies these concepts to real-world word problems:

  • Calculating total time required for one million recitations (converting seconds to minutes to hours to days)
  • Determining the time it takes sunlight to reach Earth using the speed of light ($3 \times 10^8$ m/s) and distance from Sun to Earth

What are the key conversion factors for time? $1 \text{ minute} = 60 \text{ seconds}$, $1 \text{ hour} = 60 \text{ minutes} = 3600 \text{ seconds}$, $1 \text{ day} = 24 \text{ hours} = 86400 \text{ seconds}$.
